About me

About me.

On paper I'm a content strategist and script writer. In practice I study consumer psychology. Content is just the surface I express it through. Sales, product, GTM are the other surfaces. Same skill, different canvas.

The whole game, for me, is figuring out three things about a stranger. What pulls them in. What keeps them there. What makes them act. Attention, retention, conversion. Every decision I make on a page, a script, a launch plan is one of those three, or the connective tissue between them.

Most people study content or sales or product. I study the human underneath all of them. Why they scroll, why they stop, why they trust, why they buy, why they come back, why they don't. Once you understand the behaviour, the surface layer is downstream.

I've helped creators and brands go from invisible to unmissable. Accounts grown from zero to 231K+ followers. Over 700 million views generated across platforms. Individual pieces of content crossing the 1M threshold as a regular thing, not a fluke.

But views are the receipt, not the skill. The skill is knowing why a hook works before I write it. Why one thumbnail out-clicks another before I test it. Why a landing page converts at 4% instead of 1%. Why a first-time user churns before the second session. Behaviour is predictable when you actually watch it. Most people don't watch. They optimise.

A few of the patterns I've catalogued over the last few years. Pattern breaks earn attention, familiar registers earn trust. People buy when the risk of not buying feels higher than the risk of trying. Curiosity gaps outlast information. Recognition is a free hook. Specificity kills algorithm friction. Nobody wants to feel sold, everybody wants to feel understood. Content that ignores any of these is content that gets scrolled past.

Every script I write is built to perform, not just to sound good. Hook, build, payoff. Nothing on the page is there because it sounded nice. It's there because it earns attention or it doesn't ship.

SEO is not enough anymore. Content has to be AEO ready too, discoverable by search engines and AI answer engines both. The people who win the next five years will be the ones building for both a search bar and a prompt bar.

Here's the honest paradox. I write scripts for a living, but my attention span is under three seconds. I zone out in three seconds. Who better to know whether a piece will actually hold, than the worst possible viewer.

The belief

AI ruined the content world.
Art is how we take it back.

This is not just an aesthetic argument. It is a consumer psychology one. Every human is now trained to recognise the shape of AI writing within a sentence or two, and once they clock it, trust drops before the second paragraph. Machine content isn't failing because it's bad. It's failing because the reader's guard goes up the moment they suspect no one is on the other side.

Content is an art. Art comes only from human intelligence and creativity. Every script here is generated researched, written, and rewritten by a human. Hook to CTA.

I don't generate. I make.

The feed is drowning in machine made noise. Frictionless, soulless, forgettable. I refuse to add to that pile. If you want words that were felt before they were written, that's what I do.

Here's what the AI content boom actually did. It made the average piece easier to produce and infinitely harder to remember. Everyone can now make good enough. Good enough gets scrolled past because the reader's brain filters for surprise, not accuracy. When every piece looks the same, the pattern breaker wins the whole feed.

Attention isn't a resource anymore, it's a currency, and the exchange rate on generic content is collapsing. What still trades at full value is a piece of writing that reads like a person, argues like a person, breaks a pattern like a person. Machines can't do the last part yet. That's the whole trade.

The behavioural rule underneath all of this. Humans buy from humans. They watch humans, they trust humans, they refer humans. The moment content stops feeling human, the entire consumer decision funnel above it stalls. Which means AI content isn't just a taste problem, it's a conversion problem.

My rule is simple. If I couldn't defend every sentence in a room full of my toughest readers, it doesn't ship. That filter kills the noise before anyone else has to sit through it.

ElectricPe

Complete end to end market entry strategy for ElectricPe's EV Mobility Centre in Hyderabad. Not a slide deck, a real 3 year rollout plan with unit economics, market sizing, LTV to CAC modelling, and a month by month execution roadmap.

What went into it. Market analysis of the Hyderabad EV opportunity. Sizing that separated TAM from SAM from the SOM actually reachable in year one. Customer segmentation into commercial fleets, last mile delivery, and personal EVs, each with a different acquisition motion. Why I built it this deep. Most GTM plans stop at "here's the audience, here's the channel." That's the easy half. The hard half is the sequencing. Which zone first, which segment first, which channel first, and what does month seven look like if month three didn't hit. That's what this covers. Numbers are estimates, but the reasoning is the actual product.

Live product · The one that matters

MindCare

AI counselling for mental health. A companion that feels like a friend, works like a therapist. This is not a side project. This is the thing I'm going to finish no matter how long it takes.

Why this one. I was depressed in college for a few months. I had people around me. It didn't help. That's the part most people don't understand until they've been there. You can be surrounded and still be alone with what's happening inside your head.

I got out. Not everyone does. That fact doesn't leave me alone.

The two options India offers a young person in that state are both broken. ChatGPT butters you, tells you what you want to hear, has no clinical instinct, sometimes makes it worse. Offline therapy is 1,500 to 5,000 rupees a session in the cities that even have therapists, which is not most of India. So the youth are stuck between a chatbot that flatters and a service they can't afford. That's a market failure with a body count.

What MindCare is designed to do. Not replace a therapist. Sit between you and the moment where you'd otherwise spiral alone. Trained on real counselling frameworks, not general LLM training. Feels like a friend on purpose because that's what activation actually needs. Most mental health apps lose users because the second the app feels clinical, users feel like patients, and no one wants to feel like a patient. Churn is a positioning bug, not a product bug. I'm building the opposite.

Why I know I'll finish it. Because Indian youth needs this and I know what it feels like when it's you. If MindCare saves one life, it's justified. If it scales, it saves millions. That's not marketing language for me. That's the reason this exists.

I have too many goals to hold, and I keep raising the bar on myself. I've left good opportunities. I've been through bad situations. I've always got out. That's the muscle I'm training. Not fearing loss.

Depending on which room I choose to walk into next. I'm choosy on purpose. I don't want to work on a wrapper for a fake valuation number. I want to do real work on a real market with a real thesis behind it.

AI is getting funded heavily. Doesn't matter. Most of it is going to die inside two years. I want something unique. Something that earns its place in the market the way Netflix earned India, the way luxury brands cracked a price sensitive market without lowering themselves. Those are the studies I keep going back to.

I'm not building anything. I'm an explorer.

I chase what will give me vibes and challenges. That's the compass. Titles will follow the compass, not the other way around.

The bets I'd place, that not enough people are placing.

  • Long form content is coming back. It looks like it's burning. It isn't. In the fast pace of AI, authentic long form value is what will keep us alive. Everyone can produce short. Almost no one can hold you for twenty minutes. That's the arbitrage. I'm starting my own channel to prove it.
  • Attention isn't dying. Observation is. People have stopped watching the world. Whoever can still notice, still name a feeling nobody else has put words on, gets paid. That's a market and nobody is talking about it yet.
  • Everything worth making now will be made by a human, on purpose. The premium on that is only going up. In five years "human made" will be a label on content the way "handmade" is on furniture.
  • India is the market of the decade, and most global brands don't understand it yet. Whoever cracks price-sensitive positioning without lowering the brand wins the next generation. That's a study I'm quietly running on the side.
  • Mental health is not a product category, it's an infrastructure gap. The next serious wave of Indian consumer tech is going to be built around emotional accessibility, and MindCare is my early answer to that.

If you're building something real and you want a founding brain who thinks in stories, systems, and the three seconds before a thumb moves, that's the room I'm looking for next.
